Wetterbank® is a bench that starts conversations between strangers, by helping them file complaints about the weather.

A design fiction asking how autonomous systems will shape our encounters in public space, as they move from our homes onto our streets.

Wetterbank® is a design fiction and an ongoing research project. The bench does not exist. Yet.

How it adds up.

Three things everyone at a German tram stop already knows.

  1. The weather is the safest way to start a conversation.
  2. +The tram is late. Again.
  3. +Nobody at the stop says a word.
  4. =A bench that speaks first.

Wetterbank® started as a design fiction on my portfolio. This site is where it continues.

The question.

How do interactive and autonomous systems shape social encounters in public space?

  1. Want. Do strangers at a tram stop want to talk at all? And if they do, to each other or to the bench?
  2. Help. When a bench speaks first, does it open a conversation between people, or quietly replace it?
  3. Future. How will we respond once talking objects are an ordinary part of public space, and should they be?

From a distance, A and B look the same: someone at the bench is talking. But only B is what Wetterbank® is for. A bench you chat with is just a smart speaker outdoors. So every study records the two separately, and compares both with an ordinary bench that says nothing.

Famous benches.

Ah, if only they could talk.

  • Forrest Gump 1994

    A bus stop bench in Savannah. He tells his whole life story to strangers, who let their buses go.

    Wetterbank® would have said: “Your bus left. Please continue.”

  • Good Will Hunting 1997

    A park bench in Boston. A therapist and a student, one long conversation, no small talk at all.

    Wetterbank® would have said: “Session extended. No extra charge.”

  • Manhattan 1979

    A bench by the Queensboro Bridge. Two people talk until the sun comes up.

    Wetterbank® would have said: “Overtime noted. Lovely dawn, though.”

  • La La Land 2016

    A bench above Los Angeles at dusk. Famous on the poster. It was a film set the whole time.

    Wetterbank® would have said: “Between us, I’m not even real.”

  • Paterson 2016

    A bench by the falls. A bus driver writes poems on his lunch break.

    Wetterbank® would have said: “Nice poem. Any complaints about the weather?”

  • (500) Days of Summer 2009

    A bench above downtown LA. Where they meet, and later where they say goodbye.

    Wetterbank® would have said: “Same bench, two endings. I keep the records.”

The bench in films is not just any piece of furniture: it witnesses the beginning or the end of a love story, is the set of the greatest revelations or a place of profound inspiration. (after Domus, 2019)

Decades in the front row of love, loss and genius, and not one of them ever said “Nice weather, isn’t it?”. Amateurs.
